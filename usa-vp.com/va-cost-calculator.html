How It Works

How We Calculate the True Cost

The calculator adds up five costs that come with every in-house hire:

  1. Salary: the base pay you offer.
  2. Payroll taxes and benefits: employer payroll taxes, health insurance, retirement contributions and paid time off, entered as a percentage of salary.
  3. Recruiting and onboarding: counted once for the first hire, plus your expected turnover rate multiplied by the same cost, since some hires leave and must be replaced.
  4. Equipment, software and overhead: the tools and administration each employee needs.
  5. Management time: the hours you spend directing, checking and correcting work, multiplied by what your time is worth.

"Per paid hour" divides the yearly total by 2,080 hours (40 hours × 52 weeks). Actual productive hours are lower once vacation, holidays and sick days are taken into account, so the real cost per productive hour is higher.

The Costs Most Businesses Miss

  • Turnover: every departure restarts recruiting, training and ramp-up, and loses what that person knew about your processes.
  • Coverage gaps: when one employee is out, their work usually stops.
  • Founder time: hours spent managing tasks are hours not spent on sales, clients or strategy.
  • Single points of failure: processes that live in one person's head are hard to scale.

When Outsourcing Makes Sense

Outsourcing is usually the better choice when the work is ongoing and process-driven, such as operations, CRM and pipeline management, admin and back-office tasks, and when you want capacity that can grow without another hiring cycle.
